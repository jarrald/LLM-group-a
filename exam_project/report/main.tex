\section*{Abstract}
\small
StudyGraph is a working MVP that combines a locally running language model, Chroma retrieval over course material, and configurable prompts to help students explain, summarise, and practise course content. We describe its Streamlit--LangGraph--Ollama architecture and its explicit use of Traits, Task, Tone, and Target. A separate regression strand examines what retrieval can---and cannot---supply. In an archived exploratory prediction run, linear regression achieved MAE 13.28 on three held-out cases versus 107.15 for a local LLM with RAG. In the later controlled code-generation experiment, none of six small-model programs executed, so no prediction MAE exists for that experiment. The results support a bounded conclusion: retrieval can provide relevant context, but it does not guarantee correct task execution or turn a generative model into a fitted numerical model.
\normalsize

\section{Introduction and research questions}
Students must navigate slides, notes, and examples whose terminology may differ from a general assistant's pretrained knowledge. StudyGraph addresses this with local inference and answers linked to retrieved course sources. A separate regression exercise applies the same broader idea---supplying external knowledge to an LLM---to numerical prediction and code generation. RAG here means retrieving external context before generation, in the general tradition of Lewis et al.~\cite{lewis2020rag}, not reproducing their fine-tuned architecture. We ask:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*:},leftmargin=3.2em]
\item How do local inference, RAG, and the 4T's combine in StudyGraph to provide useful, traceable study assistance?
\item What does the recorded numerical comparison show about linear regression versus LLM~+~RAG, and what are its evidential limits?
\item What do the controlled with/without-RAG runs reveal about smaller models' use of retrieved documentation and the limits of our methodology?
\end{enumerate}
