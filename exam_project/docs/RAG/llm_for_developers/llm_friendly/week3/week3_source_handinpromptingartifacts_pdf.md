Literature Review on LLM Prompting and Societal Implications
Societal aspects and potential impacts, of Al in training, inference, and prompting of Large Language Models
This study group exercise is a part of the course LLMs for Developers (Societal Aspects). The exercise focuses on how interaction
with LLMs through prompting can influence the quality, reliability, and societal impact of Al-generated information. The project
examines three challenges: ambiguity and semantic drift, response hallucination, and prompt injection.
Background
LLMs increasingly influence communication, learning, software development, and knowledge discovery.
This project examines how ambiguity, hallucination, and prompt injection affect the trustworthiness of Al
systems and their impact on individuals, organisations, and society.

Learning Objectives
Search and evaluate from literature:
e
Explain the concepts of ambiguity, semantic drift, hallucination and prompt injection.
e
Synthesize evidence from literature, in the context of training and inference (prompting) of LLMs.
e
Discuss potential implications of these potential prompting artifacts.
e
Communicate your findings to peers: assume something like a company setting where your group is
in the IT department, reporting to the CEO, CSO and CHRO. Reporting is to the class-room, of course.

blogs, and reviews, preferably from the last 5-7 years. Some inspiration on ItsLearning: articles and links.
